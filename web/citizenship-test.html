The Citizenship Practice Test

i

These are the civics questions from the naturalization interview. All of them, from the government's own list.

Just answer the questions. Type them or pick them. The page keeps track. A question is learned when you answer it right two times.

Turn Options on to get two more controls. Typing sets how often you write the answer yourself. Test switches between practice and a full 20-question test. Each one has its own i button.

This is the 2025 test. It has 128 questions. The officer asks 20 of them. You need 12 right to pass.

Filed your N‑400 before 20 October 2025?

Then you take the 2008 test. It has 100 questions. The officer asks 10. You need 6 right to pass. This page has the wrong questions for you. Go to the USCIS 2008 test page instead. We plan to add that test here later.

Options
i

A question is learned when you answer it right two times, on two separate turns. One right answer can be a lucky guess. Asking again shows whether you really know it. If you get it wrong, the count goes back to zero.

Typing
i

Questions come two ways.

Multiple choice gives you five answers. One of them is right.

Typing asks you to write the answer first. Then it shows you every answer that counts. This is closer to the real interview. There, nobody gives you a list.

This sets how often you type.

21 questions always use typing. For those, every right answer is already on the list. There is no fair wrong answer to show next to it. Naming an American Indian tribe is one example. So Less means less typing. It does not mean none.

We never mark your typing right or wrong. Many different words can be right. If we marked a right answer wrong, that would teach you the wrong thing.

Write your answer first. Then look at the answers. Then you decide whether you had it.

How would you do in the real interview? Twenty questions. You type every answer. No help. Then you see the answers and mark your own.
